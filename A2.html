Part 2: SIFT time!!

Here we had to take a set of images provided, extract the keypoints and descriptions for each and do the following:

Matches between consequetive images

For this, we had to find the matches between each consequitive pair of images and then calculate the "total cost" for the matches among the consequitive pairs.
Here I took total cost to be total distance (cost) between each matched feature pair between each image pair, summed over all image pairs and match pairs.
The average distance between two matched featurs between 2 consequitive images was 256.03, and the total distances summed for all matches for all consequitive pairs was 4608.60.

Next we were to do the same for 19 random pairs of images.
Here the average distance between two matched features between two random images was 291.84, and the total distance between matched features over all random image pairs was 100075575.50.
Here we see that the total and average costs between consequetive images compared to random images was a lot less. This likely because the images were taken in real time order, depicting the camera moving around the mulch bags with each next image. Therefore, the consequetive images are most similar to than to a random pair, as the consequetive images were taken closer together in time and space.
Due to this, the consequetive images would have more similar, closely matching features compared to a pair of random images, since the amount of real world rotation and camera movement between them would be less. This results in less "cost" of differences between matched features in consequetive images compared to non-consequetive images.
